# NexLabs Technology — Brand Voice & Public Copy

**Status:** `CANONICAL_WO_014`

## Voice

NexLabs public writing should be:
- concise;
- technically literate;
- calm;
- ambitious;
- evidence-aware;
- precise;
- human-readable.

## Tone

Sound like a serious technology company building difficult things, not:
- a hype-driven crypto launch;
- an AI influencer;
- a corporate bureaucracy;
- a science-fiction trailer with no substance.

## Copy hierarchy

Prefer:
1. problem/outcome;
2. why it matters;
3. evidence/differentiation;
4. technical mechanism;
5. detail.

## Public factual states

Content may describe:
- what NexLabs is;
- what it is building;
- approved focus areas;
- architecture/method;
- research approach;
- verified product/project state;
- public-safe evidence.

Content must not turn:
- goals into achievements;
- prototypes into deployed products;
- research directions into breakthroughs;
- internal projects into external client work;
- forecasts into actual traction.

## Claims

The Commercial Claims & Trust Policy remains authoritative.

Never fabricate:
- customers;
- testimonials;
- partners;
- patents;
- awards;
- certifications;
- team size;
- revenue;
- usage;
- market share;
- ROI;
- geographic reach.

## Technical language

Use technical terms when they clarify.

Do not add “AI-native”, “agentic”, “blockchain”, “quantum”, “real-time” or similar labels as ornamental prestige.

## Calls to action

CTAs should describe the actual available action.

Do not use a working “Book demo” or “Contact sales” CTA if the governed destination/channel does not exist.

## English / localization

English may serve as canonical public V1 language when approved by the website program.

Future localization must preserve meaning and proof status rather than merely translating words.
