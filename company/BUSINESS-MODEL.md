# NexLabs Technology — Business Model

**Status:** `CANONICAL_WO_004`

## 1. Economic model

NexLabs is an **owned-product technology company**. Its primary economic objective is to create durable software assets that can produce recurring or repeatable value without requiring proportional growth in human labor.

The company compounds economically through:
1. owned products that generate revenue and market knowledge;
2. reusable internal technology that reduces the cost and risk of future products;
3. distribution, data, workflows and operating knowledge that improve future product economics.

## 2. Primary orientation

The default revenue orientation is:

`Owned recurring product revenue → usage/API revenue → technology licensing → bounded strategic services`

This is an order of preference, not a promise that every product will use every model.

## 3. Core revenue families

### A. Subscription software
Recurring access to SaaS, applications, tools or premium product capabilities.

Use when:
- the product creates ongoing value;
- customer need is recurring;
- continued operation/support is part of the value.

### B. Usage-based / metered software
Revenue tied to API calls, AI generations, compute, data volume, automation runs or another measurable unit.

Use when:
- cost-to-serve scales materially with usage;
- customer value scales with usage;
- a clear usage unit exists.

### C. Hybrid subscription + usage
A recurring base tier plus included usage and paid overage/credits.

Use when:
- customers value predictable access;
- variable AI/compute/data cost needs protection;
- usage varies meaningfully between accounts.

### D. Transaction / platform fees
A fee tied to transactions, marketplace activity, settlement, commerce or another platform event.

Use only when:
- NexLabs genuinely facilitates the transaction;
- legal/compliance/security requirements are satisfied;
- the fee is transparent and economically aligned.

### E. Licensing
Licensing of APIs, engines, SDKs, internal technology or proprietary workflows when external licensing creates more value than keeping the capability purely internal.

### F. Enterprise agreements
Contracted access, support, deployment, security/compliance features or negotiated capacity for business customers.

Enterprise pricing must not become disguised bespoke software development unless that is explicitly admitted.

### G. Bounded strategic services
Selective advisory, implementation, research or engineering may exist only when it:
- accelerates a NexLabs product or reusable technology;
- creates strategically valuable learning;
- has attractive economics;
- does not consume the company into project-by-project client labor.

Generic outsourced development is not the default business model.

## 4. Portfolio economics

Each product is expected to justify continued investment through a combination of:
- user/customer value;
- recurring or repeatable revenue potential;
- gross/contribution margin potential;
- retention or repeat usage;
- acquisition/distribution viability;
- strategic learning or reusable technology;
- acceptable risk and support burden.

A product may be strategically useful before it is profitable, but that status must remain explicit.

## 5. Revenue quality hierarchy

All else equal, NexLabs prefers revenue that is:
1. recurring or repeatable;
2. high-margin after AI/cloud/data cost;
3. low in manual service burden;
4. diversified across customers;
5. contractually and technically predictable;
6. aligned with durable product value;
7. compliant, transparent and reversible where appropriate.

## 6. What does not count as a core business model

The following do not become company revenue strategies by default:
- speculative crypto or market trading;
- one-time windfalls;
- token price appreciation;
- unverified affiliate schemes;
- selling user data without explicit lawful product rationale;
- ad inventory that materially degrades user trust;
- perpetual custom engineering that cannot be productized.

## 7. Product-specific authority

This document sets company-level economic architecture. A product still needs its own validated pricing/monetization decision before launch.

No product-specific price is canonical merely because a generic range or idea appeared in a conversation.
