# NexLabs Technology — Iterate, Scale, Pause, Pivot & Kill

**Status:** `CANONICAL_WO_008`

## Principle

Continued investment must be earned by evidence or explicit strategic learning.

Past effort is not a reason to continue.

## ITERATE

Choose when:
- core problem/value still looks valid;
- evidence reveals specific solvable gaps;
- another bounded experiment can materially reduce uncertainty.

Iteration should have a hypothesis.

## SCALE

Choose when evidence supports materially increasing:
- traffic/distribution;
- infrastructure;
- product breadth;
- commercial spend;
- operating capacity.

Scale requires more than usage spikes.

Relevant evidence may include:
- retention;
- repeat value;
- conversion/revenue;
- acceptable cost-to-serve;
- reliability;
- support burden;
- distribution efficiency.

## MAINTAIN

Choose when:
- product is useful/economic;
- large investment is not currently justified;
- obligations/support continue.

## PAUSE

Choose when:
- capacity is needed elsewhere;
- evidence is insufficient but optionality is valuable;
- dependency/timing blocks progress;
- economics/risk need later reassessment.

Pause should define a revisit trigger.

## PIVOT

Choose when:
- evidence invalidates a core user/problem/value/distribution hypothesis;
- a materially stronger adjacent hypothesis emerges.

Pivot is not cosmetic rebranding.

Changed assumptions require revalidation.

## KILL / RETIRE

Choose when:
- problem/value is weak;
- distribution is structurally poor;
- economics are unattractive;
- risk is unacceptable;
- quality cannot reach threshold;
- strategic fit disappears;
- opportunity cost dominates.

## Sunk cost

“Already spent months building it” is not a positive scoring factor.

Only future expected value, obligations and strategic learning matter.

## Kill quality

A good shutdown:
- protects users/data;
- stops billing appropriately;
- archives evidence/lessons;
- releases resources;
- preserves reusable components only when actually useful.
