# NexLabs Technology — Revenue Architecture

**Status:** `CANONICAL_WO_004`

## Revenue stack

### Layer 1 — Core recurring product revenue
Highest strategic priority.

Examples:
- SaaS subscription;
- consumer/prosumer subscription;
- team/business plan;
- premium product capability.

### Layer 2 — Variable usage revenue
Protects unit economics where variable infrastructure/AI cost matters.

Examples:
- API calls;
- generations;
- automation runs;
- compute/data processing;
- storage or bandwidth where appropriate.

### Layer 3 — Platform / transaction revenue
Only for products where NexLabs creates measurable transactional infrastructure value.

Examples:
- marketplace fee;
- transaction/service fee;
- settlement-related platform fee where legally permitted.

### Layer 4 — Licensing
Monetizes reusable technology without requiring a full end-user application.

Examples:
- SDK;
- API;
- engine;
- internal platform capability;
- enterprise license.

### Layer 5 — Enterprise contracts
Adds predictable larger contracts where product maturity supports them.

May include:
- capacity;
- advanced governance/security;
- SLA/support;
- deployment patterns;
- enterprise administration.

### Layer 6 — Strategic services
Lowest-priority recurring operating model and deliberately bounded.

Services should exist to:
- accelerate product adoption;
- fund/prove productization;
- learn a high-value workflow;
- support a strategically important enterprise use case.

They should not become a substitute for building scalable products.

## Revenue concentration

No product or customer concentration threshold is frozen in WO-004. Later Finance/GTM WOs must define monitoring and acceptable concentration ranges using real portfolio data.

## Currency / geography

NexLabs may operate across local and international markets. Currency, tax, payment-provider and entity decisions are product/jurisdiction-specific and are not frozen here.

## Product mapping

A product should normally have:
- one **primary** revenue model;
- at most a small number of supporting models;
- explicit reasons for each model;
- measured costs and risks.

Revenue complexity is not a feature.

## Portfolio principle

Different products may monetize differently, but every model must fit the same company guardrails:
- understandable value exchange;
- measurable economics;
- transparent customer impact;
- acceptable compliance/security risk;
- low dependence on manual labor where possible.
